Why APCA scores differently

Three things make APCA perceptual where WCAG 2 is mechanical. First, it models how eyes respond to lightness — mid-grays lose more than the ratio suggests. Second, it treats polarity asymmetrically: light text on a dark background is judged by a different curve than dark text on light, which is why the same pair can score +106 one way and −108 the other. Third, the passing bar moves with font size: small body text needs Lc 75, a 24px heading only needs Lc 60. WCAG 2 has one bar for all text; APCA has a bar per use.

How to use this honestly

  1. Ship to WCAG 2 today. APCA is a draft for WCAG 3. The 4.5:1 rule is still what auditors and the law check — use the contrast checker for that.
  2. Use APCA as a second opinion. When WCAG 2 barely passes (4.5–5:1) or when the pair is light-on-dark, check the Lc. If APCA is under 75 for body text, darken the text or lighten the background even though WCAG 2 says it passes.
  3. Trust the disagreement on dark mode. Light grays on near-black are the classic case where WCAG 2 over-promises. If your dark theme text sits at #9CA3AF or lighter on #121212, APCA is telling you it reads worse than the AAA label suggests.

How the math works

APCA 0.98G: sRGB channels are linearized, combined into relative luminance Y (0.2126729 R + 0.7151522 G + 0.0721750 B), soft-clamped near black (threshold 0.022), then raised to polarity-dependent powers (0.56/0.57 for dark-on-light, 0.65/0.62 for light-on-dark), scaled by 1.14 and reported as Lc × 100. FAQ

Is APCA the new WCAG standard?

Not yet. APCA is the contrast method proposed for WCAG 3 and is still a draft. WCAG 2.1 contrast ratios (4.5:1 for normal text) are the rules that govern compliance today. Use APCA as a second opinion that is closer to how human vision actually perceives contrast, not as a replacement for the WCAG 2 check.

Why is the APCA number sometimes negative?

The sign shows polarity, not quality: a positive Lc means dark text on a light background, a negative Lc means light text on a dark background. Only the absolute value matters for readability — Lc −84 and Lc +84 are equally readable.

Why does APCA disagree with the WCAG 2 ratio on my pair?

APCA models human perception: it weighs lightness the way eyes do, treats dark-on-light and light-on-dark differently, and ties the required score to font size. WCAG 2 uses one fixed ratio for all text. The two methods usually agree on black and white, but they can disagree sharply on mid-grays — for example light gray #9CA3AF on near-black #121212 scores 7.38:1 (AAA) under WCAG 2 but only Lc −52.9 under APCA, below even the large-text line.

What Lc value do I need?

Under the APCA 0.98G draft guidance: Lc 75 is the minimum for body text (90 is preferred), Lc 60 for large text (24px and up), Lc 45 for large bold text, and Lc 30 for non-text elements such as icons, borders, and focus rings. Anything below Lc 15 is effectively unreadable.
